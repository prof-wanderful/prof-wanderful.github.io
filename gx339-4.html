GX 339-4: Broadband Spectral–Timing Study

Multi-mission X-ray analysis of the 2023–2024 outburst of the black hole X-ray binary GX 339-4

This page accompanies my poster presentation on the broadband spectral–timing evolution and high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy of GX 339-4 during its 2023–2024 outburst.

Project Overview

GX 339-4 is one of the most extensively studied black hole X-ray binaries and provides an excellent laboratory for investigating accretion physics, state transitions, disk–corona coupling, and reflection from the inner accretion flow.

In this work, we study selected epochs from the 2023–2024 outburst using coordinated observations from multiple X-ray missions. The selected observations trace the source from an intermediate state close to the HIMS/SIMS boundary, through a soft-intermediate state with a narrow low-frequency QPO, and finally into a disk-dominated soft state.

Observatories and Instruments

  • NICER
  • NuSTAR
  • AstroSat / SXT and LAXPC
  • INTEGRAL / ISGRI and SPI
  • XMM-Newton / EPIC-pn and RGS

Analysis Highlights

  • Broadband spectral fitting was performed using a common physical model including disk emission, thermal Comptonization, relativistic reflection, absorption, and partial covering.
  • Epochs 1 and 2 show broadly similar time-averaged spectral shapes, with only moderate changes in the Comptonized and reflected components.
  • Timing diagnostics provide the key distinction between these epochs, with a clear low-frequency QPO detected during Epoch 2.
  • In the later soft-state epoch, the Comptonized emission and reflection contribution are substantially reduced, consistent with a disk-dominated spectral state and suppressed rapid variability.
  • High-resolution RGS spectra were examined to search for narrow features around the Fe-L/Ne region.

Spectral Model

The broadband spectra were fitted using the model:

constant × edge × pcfabs × TBabs × (thcomp × diskbb + relxillCp)

This model allows us to describe the thermal disk emission, Comptonized continuum, relativistic reflection, and absorption-related spectral complexity in a consistent framework across the selected epochs.
